\section{Proposed extension: merge, migrate a free center, and split}
\label{sec:merge-migrate-split}
\textbf{Discussion recorded 29 September 2026; not implemented or tested.}
This extension concerns the later bounded Voronoi experiment, not the historical
shared-vertex hexagon annealer. The user proposed the merge trigger and migration
mechanism below and requested that they be documented before implementation.
The split optimization and geometric conventions remain proposals for discussion.
No results from older runs establish the performance of this extension.

\subsection{Context: the current Voronoi weight and grade}
The domain $P$ is the convex hull of the verified Manhattan main-island region.
The current experiment uses 100 sites and all 5,986 pickup records in that hull,
retaining coincident records as separate observations. Cells are Euclidean
Voronoi regions clipped to $P$. Neighbors share a positive-length edge.
At the start of a movement calculation, let $M_j$ be the current pickup count,
$A_j^0$ the current cell area, and
\[
 \rho_j=\frac{M_j}{A_j^0},\qquad
 \widehat W_j(q)=\rho_j A_j(q).
\]
Densities are frozen throughout that calculation. Candidate weights are real-valued
modeled weights; they are not post-move pickup counts. Counts can be refreshed
for the next selection, but must not be substituted into acceptance tests along
the current move.

For area $A_j$ and perimeter $L_j$, circularity and mean neighbor gap are
\[
 C_j=\frac{4\pi A_j}{L_j^2},\qquad
 \widehat G_j=\frac{1}{|N_j|}\sum_{k\in N_j}
          |\widehat W_j-\widehat W_k|.
\]
The current combined badness, with fixed reference weight
$\overline W=5986/100=59.86$, is
\[
 B_j=1-C_j+\frac{\widehat G_j}{59.86}.
\]
Lower badness is better. This puts circularity loss and normalized weight gap
on a common dimensionless scale, with equal coefficients. Existing movement
choices seek circularity improvement or modeled-gap improvement, selected with
equal probability, and use the moving cell's combined grade for acceptance.
New neighbors are allowed in this later experiment.

The current selection rule samples with replacement, with probability
$B_j/\sum_k B_k$ after refreshing the selection grades; use uniform selection
if all badness values vanish. A batch comprises 100 selections, so it may select
some centers repeatedly and omit others. This differs from the earlier rounds
of random permutations, which gave every center exactly one turn.

\subsection{User-proposed merge trigger}
For a selected cell $i$, choose a lightest neighbor
\[
 j_*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{j\in N_i} W_j,\qquad
 T_i=\operatorname{median}\{W_k:k\in\{i\}\cup N_i\}.
\]
The proposed trigger is the strict inequality
\[
 \boxed{W_i+W_{j_*}<T_i.}
\]
The median includes $i$ and all its neighbors, and averages the two middle
values when their number is even. Equality does not trigger a merge. Here $W$
denotes the current weight state used to make this decision, consistent with
the density-based model above; a separate rule for refreshing that state during
a multi-stage relocation still needs to be fixed.

When triggered, the user's proposal is to combine these two cells into one,
keep one center active, and mark the other as a \emph{free center}. The free
center does not generate a Voronoi cell while migrating. Which center survives,
and whether the surviving center moves during merging, remain undecided.
A deterministic tie convention is also needed for equally light neighbors.

\paragraph{Geometric issue that must be settled before implementation.}
The exact union of two adjacent Voronoi cells is generally not itself a cell
of an ordinary Euclidean Voronoi diagram; it may even be nonconvex. Simply
deleting one site can give portions of its old region to several surviving
neighbors, not exclusively to $j_*$. Thus there are two distinct possibilities:
retain ordinary Voronoi geometry and interpret the merge as site deletion with
local redistribution, or require an exact two-cell union and introduce a
representation that can retain it. Neither choice has been adopted here.
The implementation must not silently equate deletion with an exact merge.

\subsection{User-proposed migration to a local maximum}
Associate the free center with a host cell and repeatedly route it to the
host's heaviest neighbor, until it reaches a local maximum in weight. This is
a discrete routing operation; it need not move an active site or change any
cell boundary during the route.

A proposed precise convention is to move from host $h$ to a heaviest neighbor
only when that neighbor has strictly greater weight than $h$, stopping when
\[
 W_h\geq \max_{k\in N_h}W_k.
\]
With fixed weights and adjacency, every hop then strictly increases weight,
so a finite graph cannot contain a routing cycle. This guarantee does not
apply if weights or adjacency change while routing. The initial host, whether
to freeze the routing state, tie handling, and treatment of equal-weight
plateaus must be agreed. Routing several free centers concurrently would also
require a conflict rule; sequential processing is a possible implementation,
not an adopted requirement.

\subsection{Proposed local optimization of the split}
At the destination cell $h$, reactivate the free center. The existing center
and the free center become two distinct sites $p,q$ generating the two child
cells. Keep all other site positions fixed while optimizing these four scalar
coordinates jointly. Optimizing only the new site's position would leave out
half of the requested placement problem.

A candidate initialization is a small symmetric separation around the old
center, trying several directions, including the destination cell's longest
direction. Both sites must remain distinct and inside $P$. Separation size,
clearance, direction sampling, and handling of a center close to the boundary
are still numerical design choices. Symmetric placement is an initialization
proposal, not a constraint on the optimized pair.

\paragraph{Candidate local objective, not yet adopted.}
Let $\mathcal S$ include the two children and the neighboring cells whose
grades are to be protected. One proposed objective is
\[
 \Phi(p,q)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}
 \left(1-C_j(p,q)+\frac{\widehat G_j(p,q)}{59.86}\right).
\]
Minimizing this sum allows a loss in one component when the improvement in the
other is larger on the agreed scale. Including neighbors extends acceptance
beyond the previous moving-cell-only rule and therefore requires explicit
agreement. All neighbors needed to evaluate each $\widehat G_j$ must be
included in the evaluation data, even when their sites remain fixed. Fixed
sites can still have changing cell areas and grades.

The affected neighborhood must be defined consistently when topology changes:
either optimize within a fixed local scope with appropriate event restrictions,
or expand and re-evaluate the scope when another cell becomes affected. It is
not valid to compare objective sums over different sets without accounting for
the added terms. This locality convention remains open.

\paragraph{Candidate conservative density model for the split.}
The existing formula $\widehat W_j=\rho_j A_j$ attaches density to a cell's
identity. Merging and creating identities make its extension ambiguous; it
also does not generally conserve the total modeled weight when adjacent
cells with different densities exchange area. A proposed alternative for this
operation freezes a \emph{spatial} density field on the pre-operation regions:
\[
 \rho_0(x)=\rho_k\quad(x\in V_k^0),\qquad
 \widehat W_j(p,q)=\int_{V_j(p,q)}\rho_0(x)\,dx
   =\sum_k\rho_k\,|V_j(p,q)\cap V_k^0|.
\]
This conserves modeled total weight when the candidate regions partition $P$.
It uses geometry and frozen densities rather than post-move pickup counts.
It is a proposed model change, not a description of the implemented
$\rho_j A_j$ rule. Whether to freeze the field before merging or only before
splitting, and whether to use it for later ordinary moves, must be decided.

\paragraph{Numerical optimization.}
Once the objective, density model, and scope are fixed, use joint numerical
gradients and a decreasing-step search, accepting only feasible decreases in
$\Phi$. Absolute weight gaps and Voronoi topology changes make the objective
potentially nonsmooth, so a gradient alone does not establish improvement;
evaluate the candidate objective explicitly, without recounting pickups for
acceptance. Multiple initial split directions can reduce sensitivity to one
starting arrangement. This is a local optimization proposal, with no claim of
a global optimum or convergence of the full merge--migrate--split process.
Search tolerances and stopping conditions have not yet been specified.

\subsection{Restart point, safeguards, and decision status}
The intended comparison starts again from the saved end-of-round-59 state,
before badness-weighted selection began. Preserve that checkpoint and all
later runs, figures, and documents; do not overwrite them or label a later
state as the restart. No restart or simulation change is performed by this
document update.

A completed merge-and-split transaction removes one active site and restores
one, returning to 100 active centers. There would be 99 during the inactive
interval unless the entire operation is committed atomically. Choose that
policy explicitly and preserve the pre-operation state if the split fails.
Validate distinct in-region sites, complete nonoverlapping coverage, positive
cell areas, weight accounting, and the selected geometric representation.

Before implementation, settle: exact union versus Voronoi deletion; surviving
center and initial routing host; weight refresh and routing ties; sequential
versus concurrent free centers; the split density model; the local objective
and affected scope; and numerical search/rollback rules. The merge trigger and
migration idea above record the user's proposed addition. The remaining
choices are documented alternatives, not silently agreed rules or tested
results.
